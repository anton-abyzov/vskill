# Stage map: a public 13-step toolkit against this pipeline

## Source and credit

The ordering in this skill was checked against a workflow Riley Brown showed publicly in "The Biggest Week in Claude History..." (YouTube, published November 14, 2026, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j5BDkyEcML4). In the first minutes he shows a whiteboard, "Video Editing Presentation Series", and a sheet, "The AI Video Editing Toolkit", that list the tools he uses in 13 steps from computer to finished video. He says both were generated by an agent from about 70 of his own editing threads.

What is taken from that source: the idea of writing the whole edit down as one ordered list of stages with an owner per stage, and the names of the public tools below, which are facts read off the screen. Nothing else is: his sheet, whiteboard, skills, prompts and wording are his and are not reproduced here. The video spends about three minutes on the pipeline and does not explain how any step works, so every instruction in this skill comes from our own practice. Where a row says "per his sheet", it has not been checked beyond the tool's public page.

Two tools on his sheet are public repositories worth knowing: `kevinbadi/open-edits` (listed as the home of a reference-video measuring skill) and `mirage-hq/Tesseract` (listed as a local editing CLI). Both existed on GitHub on November 14, 2026. Neither is a dependency of this skill, and `analyze.py` was written independently with ffmpeg.

## Where the two differ on purpose

- **Cutting.** Timeline-editor connectors (Tella, Descript, Premiere) cut by text or by silence. Recorded takes here follow the opposite rule: the last attempt of each passage is kept whole, pauses included, and only the head and tail of a take are trimmed. A reviewed cut list rendered by ffmpeg does that exactly and can be re-run; a text-based silence remover cannot.
- **Analysis.** His list spreads analysis across six tools. One script that writes one file is easier to resume from and to hand to another model. Face tracking and a video-understanding model are left as options because most of these videos are a full-screen speaker with cards over it, with no moving crop to track.
- **Scraping.** His step gathers material from the web. Here a downloaded video is a reference to measure; only licensed, owned or generated media enters `assets/`.
- **3D on rented GPUs.** Not needed for the current videos, and it adds a paid service. Revisit when a video needs a rendered 3D scene that HyperFrames' own 3D route cannot produce.

## What his list had that was missing here

1. A written order with one owner per stage. The skills existed; the route between them lived in the operator's head.
2. Measuring before cutting as a fixed step, with pacing numbers.
3. Measuring a reference video instead of describing it.
4. Stating mastering targets and re-measuring the render.

Those four are what this skill adds.
